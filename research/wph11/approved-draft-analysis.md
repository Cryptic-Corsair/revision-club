Companion to `WPH11-Topic2-draft-notes.md`. This file holds the evidence trail; the draft notes deliberately carry no session references.

## 1. Scope and method

- Papers analysed: every WPH11/01 session from January 2019 to October 2025 (20 sessions, including October 2025), plus the alternative 01A papers (October 2025 01A, January 2026 01A, June 2026 01A) and the two 2026 main sessions as future-proofing extras. 25 sessions in total, 50 documents.
- June 2020 does not exist (series cancelled); June 2021 exists as "Summer 2021".
- Source: complete question papers and mark schemes (Edexcel originals, obtained via an aggregator after the Physics & Maths Tutor site proved to be behind a Cloudflare challenge). Every PDF was converted to text and verified complete (paper-end markers and word counts).
- Every question was checked against the Topic 2 (Materials) specification boundary: density, upthrust/flotation, laminar/turbulent flow, viscosity/Stokes' law/terminal velocity in a fluid, Core Practical 2, Hooke's law, stress/strain/Young modulus, force-extension graphs and named terms, stress-strain graphs and breaking stress, elastic strain energy, Core Practical 3. Terminal-velocity-in-air questions were kept out (the mark schemes themselves say "Ignore mention of upthrust" for those), unless the mark scheme itself brings in fluid reasoning.
- Five parallel analysis passes produced verbatim mark-scheme extraction, saved as `download/wph11-analysis/agent-1.md` … `agent-5.md`.

## 3. Question templates that keep returning

1. Stokes' law conditions — asked in some form in essentially every session, as a one-mark list, a two-mark structured item, or an MCQ. Accepted answer is always the same trio: small/spherical, low speed, laminar (or absence of turbulent flow).
2. "Deduce/assess whether Stokes' law applies" — six appearances with the identical chain: drag from weight − upthrust; Stokes drag 6πηrv; compare; conclude. The 2025 October 01A paper reused the exact data of the 2020 January question (same diameter, mass, upthrust, viscosity, speed).
3. Temperature-viscosity-terminal velocity — appears nearly every session in some disguise (MCQ, explain-the-time, deduce-the-change).
4. r³ against r scaling — "larger sphere: weight and upthrust grow as r³, drag as r, so terminal velocity rises" — as a 4–5 mark explanation and as the discriminating idea of a starred rising-bubble question.
5. Upthrust/flotation scenario carousel — the same physics rotates through eggs, boats, submarines, hydrometers, icebergs, cartesian divers, Galilean thermometers, lifting bags. The Galilean thermometer and iceberg scenarios each reappear years apart; the sphere-on-balance question reappears with identical numbers.
6. Core Practical 2 in disguise — a full method write-up every two to three sessions; in the other sessions the same practical appears as a drag calculation or an error-analysis item.
7. Core Practical 3 — a full wire write-up roughly every other session; otherwise fragments (diameter technique, taut-string detail, percentage-uncertainty reduction, k = EA/x).
8. Young modulus calculation chain (A = πr², σ = F/A, ε = Δx/x, E = σ/ε) — at least one complete chain in every single paper since 2019.
9. Area reasoning — area under force-extension = energy; area between loading/unloading = dissipated; area under stress-strain = energy per unit volume (multiply by volume); trapezium for a change between two forces.
10. Six-mark starred questions — always a multi-force explanation (balloon ascent, vacuum vs oil, rising bubble, Galilean thermometer) or a compare-and-contrast of two graphs (biscuit vs toffee, glue capsules). The indicative-content grid has not changed since 2019.

## 4. Are the mark schemes changing?

Core wording is stable across all seven years. These exact strings recur from 2019 to 2026: "Use of F = 6πηrv", "Use of upthrust = weight of fluid displaced", "Use of ρ = m/V", "Use of σ = F/A", "Use of ε = Δx/x", "Use of E = σ/ε", "Use of ΔF = kΔx", "Use of ΔEel = ½FΔx", "Comparison of calculated value with … and consistent conclusion", "dependent on MP1", "ecf from …", and the identical free-body-diagram penalty block (extra force, arrow not touching the dot, arrow not vertical).

What has changed is presentation, not physics:
- MCQ rationale phrasing drifted: "D is the correct answer" (2019–2020) → "The only correct answer is D because …" (2021) → "The correct answer is D" (2022) → "The only correct answer is D" plus one "is not correct because" line per distractor (2024 October onwards).
- From mid-2025 the mark schemes add an "Additional Guidance" column carrying Allow / Ignore / tolerance / dependency notes that used to sit inline.
- The starred-question grid, the show-that rules, the unit rules (missing unit costs the final calculation mark), the g = 9.81 rule and the sig-fig rule are unchanged throughout.

Conclusion for future-proofing: notes written in the stable core wording will match both current and future mark schemes. The wording drift is cosmetic and should not change the notes.

## 6. Trap list observed in mark schemes (each costs a real mark)

- Diameter used as radius in V = (4/3)πr³ or A = πr².
- cm³ not converted to m³ (divide by 10⁶); mm not converted to m.
- Final length used where extension is wanted (stiffness, work done, "new length" answers).
- Absolute values used where differences are wanted (k = ΔF/Δx between two states).
- Upthrust omitted from weight = upthrust + drag.
- The v in Stokes' law misread as a volume.
- A deduce/assess answer that stops at a number without the comparison and conclusion.
- "Measure the extension" in method questions (rejected; you measure lengths and subtract).
- "Temperature of the room" (rejected; the liquid's temperature).
- "Greater Young modulus" offered where the object's stiffness changed (rejected).
- "Mass is constant" where the mark scheme wants "weight is constant".
- Extra forces, arrows not touching the dot, or arrows off-vertical in free-body diagrams.
- "The flow was turbulent" or "wrong densities used" offered as measurement errors in the viscometer.
- Using g = 10 instead of 9.81 (one mark, once).
