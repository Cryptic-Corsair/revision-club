### October 2023 (que/rms/pef-oct2023)

**[Oct23] Q1 (1 mark, spec 4)** — Scalar vs vector classification
"Which row of the table is correct?" (Scalar | Vector): A energy / momentum; B displacement / acceleration; C time / mass; D velocity / force.
Correct: **A**. MS: "B is incorrect because displacement is a vector; C is incorrect because mass is a scaler [sic]; D is incorrect because velocity is a vector." ER: "Energy is a scalar and momentum is a vector."

**[Oct23] Q2 (1 mark, spec 13, 4)** — SI unit of momentum
Options: A kg m s⁻¹; B J m⁻¹; C m s⁻²; D N s⁻¹.
Correct: **A**. MS: "B is incorrect because this is an SI unit of force; C is incorrect because this is an SI unit of acceleration; D is incorrect because units of momentum are usually either kg m s⁻¹ or N s." ER: "Momentum = mass (kg) × velocity (m s⁻¹)."

**[Oct23] Q3 (1 mark, spec 29b)** — Elastic limit definition
"Which of the following could be used to describe the elastic limit of a material?" A: stress above which the material will not obey Hooke's law; B: stress above which the material will be deformed when the stress is removed; C: stress at which the material undergoes a sudden increase in extension; D: stress at which the material breaks.
Correct: **B**. MS: "A is incorrect because Hooke's law is when force ∝ extension; C is incorrect because the elastic limit relates to what happens when the deforming force is removed; D is incorrect because this is the breaking stress." (A = limit of proportionality, C = yield point, D = breaking stress — all four spec-29b terms in one item.)

**[Oct23] Q4 (1 mark, spec 2, 3, 1)** — Displacement–time graph of a falling ball
"A ball falls through air from rest." Pick the displacement–time graph (A: horizontal line — stationary; B: straight line through origin — constant velocity; C: curve of increasing steepness; D: curve levelling off — decreasing velocity).
Correct: **C**. MS: "A is incorrect because this would be stationary; B is incorrect because this would be constant velocity; D is incorrect because this would be a decreasing velocity." ER: "When accelerating due to gravity, negative velocity increases. The gradient of a displacement time graph represents velocity, so the gradient becomes increasingly negative."

**[Oct23] Q5 (1 mark, spec 19, 17)** — GPE as work done against gravity
"A helicopter moves vertically upwards with an increasing speed. Which of the following describes the increase in gravitational potential energy of the helicopter?" A: work done on the helicopter by the force of gravity; B: total work done by the helicopter as it moves upwards; C: work done by the helicopter against the force of gravity; D: work done by the helicopter to increase speed.
Correct: **C**. MS: "A is incorrect because the force of gravity is down and the helicopter is going up; B is incorrect because this includes increasing kinetic energy; D is incorrect because this is the increase in kinetic energy." ER: "Work done against gravity is equal to the increase in gravitational potential energy."

**[Oct23] Q6 (1 mark, spec 18)** — Kinetic energy doubled, new velocity
"An object moves with a velocity v. The kinetic energy of the object is doubled." New velocity: A √2·v; B 2v; C 2√2·v; D 4v.
Correct: **A**. MS: "Ek ∝ v² or v ∝ √Ek; if 2Ek then v(new) = √2 v." ER works it as ½m(v₂)² = 2 × ½m(v₁)² so (v₂)² = 2(v₁)², therefore v₂ = √2 v₁.

**[Oct23] Q7 (1 mark, spec 22, 21)** — Efficiency of a power station
"A power station provides an electrical output power of 2.1 GW. The power station transfers thermal energy to the surroundings at a rate of 3.4 GW." Efficiency = ? A: 2.1/(3.4+2.1); B: 3.4/(3.4+2.1); C: (3.4−2.1)/2.1; D: (3.4−2.1)/3.4.
Correct: **A** (useful output / total input, with total input = output + waste = 5.5 GW). ER: "Application of efficiency equation with total power input = 2.1 GW + 3.4 GW."

**[Oct23] Q8 (1 mark, spec 28, 27)** — Young modulus scaling with wire dimensions
"A student applied a tensile force to a metal wire of length x and diameter d. The length of the wire increased by Δx. The student applied the same force to a second wire made of the same material. The length of the second wire increased by 2Δx." Which row (Length | Diameter)? A 0.5x / 0.5d; B 0.5x / 2d; C 2x / 0.5d; D 2x / 2d.
Correct: **A**. MS: "stress ∝ strain or F/A ∝ Δx/x or Δx ∝ x/d²; if 2Δx then 0.5x/(0.5d)² = 2(x/d²)." ER derives l = 2x·d(new)²/d² and: "Substituting d(new) = 0.5d gives a corresponding new length of 0.5x."

**[Oct23] Q9 (1 mark, spec 4, 16)** — Vector equation for equilibrium
A box of weight W hangs in equilibrium from two ropes with tensions T₁ and T₂. "Which of the following is a vector expression relating the forces acting on the box?" A W + T₁ = T₂; B W + T₂ = T₁; C W = T₁ + T₂; D W + T₁ + T₂ = 0 (all as vectors).
Correct: **D**. MS: "the addition of vector forces on an object in equilibrium = 0" (A/B/C are scalar-style additions that ignore direction). ER: "The vector sum of forces in equilibrium is zero."

**[Oct23] Q10 (1 mark, spec 17)** — Work done by a force at an angle
A box is pulled 5 m along the ground by a rope inclined at 40° to the horizontal; tension in the rope 30 N. Work done on the box? A 150 J; B 115 J; C 96 J; D 23 J.
Correct: **B**. MS: "W = F cos θ × d" → 30 × cos40° × 5 = 115 J. (A omits cos θ; C uses sin θ; D is the horizontal force component only, 23 N.) ER: "Work done = 5 m × 30 N × cos(40°) = 115 J."

**[Oct23] Q11 (4 marks, spec 9, 10)** — Newton's second law on a rocket
A rocket is launched vertically; its engines give a total upward thrust of 7.3 × 10⁷ N; mass of rocket = 5.0 × 10⁶ kg.
- (a) (3) Calculate the initial acceleration. MS: "Use of W = mg (1); Use of F = ma (1); a = 4.8 m s⁻² (1)." Example calculation: W = 5.0 × 10⁶ kg × 9.81 N kg⁻¹ = 4.91 × 10⁷ N; ΣF = 7.3 × 10⁷ N − 4.91 × 10⁷ N = 2.39 × 10⁷ N; a = 2.39 × 10⁷ N / 5.0 × 10⁶ kg = 4.78 m s⁻².
- (b) (1) Reason the acceleration will increase as the rocket rises. MS: "The mass / weight of the rocket / fuel decreases (because fuel is used up) Or The thrust force increases Or The resultant force increases (1)."
ER Oct23 Q11(a): "A common mistake was to treat the thrust from the rocket's engines as the resultant force, which led to an incorrect answer." ER Q11(b): "A fair proportion of candidates explained why the rocket accelerated, by stating that the thrust was greater than the weight, rather than explaining why the acceleration would increase."

**[Oct23] Q12 (6 marks, spec 14, 13, 12)** — Conservation of momentum on an air track
Two gliders A and B of equal mass on an air track; both initially stationary, then A pushed gently towards B; magnets attached; gliders collide and stick; a data logger records the velocity of A, which after the collision is half the velocity before.
- (a) (2) State the principle of conservation of linear momentum. MS: "Total momentum before (a collision) = total momentum after (a collision) Or total momentum remains constant (1); When no external force acts Or When no resultant force acts on the system Or In a closed / isolated system (1)."
- (b)(i) (2) Deduce whether the results show the law is obeyed. MS: "Momentum is mass × velocity and after the collision the mass (that is moving) is double the original value (1); (because velocity is half its original value) momentum remains the same so the law is obeyed (dependent on MP1) (1)." OR algebraic route: equate initial momentum of A to final momentum of A + B and show consistency with v/2.
- (b)(ii) (2) Why the magnetic attraction did not affect the demonstration. MS: "(The gliders accelerate in opposite directions because) the magnetic forces are equal in size and opposite in direction Or … form a Newton's 3rd law pair (1); So the velocity of one glider increases and the velocity of the other decreases (by the same amount) Or So the resultant force on the system is zero Or The magnetic forces are not external forces (1)."
ER Oct23 Q12(b)(ii): "some answers referred to the forces cancelling out, rather than stating that the forces were equal in size and opposite in direction" and "To gain credit for an explanation involving the resultant force, it therefore had to be clear that the answer was referring to the resultant force on the system."

**[Oct23] Q14 (7 marks, spec 6, 17, 21)** — Scaled vector diagram + power of an exercise machine

**[Oct23] Q15 (8 marks, spec 16, 15)** — Centre of gravity and moments: loft ladder on a hinged board

**[Oct23] Q17 (14 marks, spec 24, 23, 9, 3)** — Weather balloon: upthrust, density and changing velocity
A weather balloon carries equipment into the upper atmosphere; upthrust acts; released from ground it accelerates upwards.

**[Jan24] Q6 (1 mark, spec 5, 16)** — Resolving tensions for a bird on a string
A string between two vertical posts; a bird of weight W stands on the middle; the string makes an angle of 80° with each post; tension T in each side. Correct expression? A T = W/(2 sin80°); B T = W × 2 sin80°; C T = W/(2 cos80°); D T = W × 2 cos80°.

**[Jan24] Q7 (1 mark, spec 27, 29a)** — Stiffness from a length–force graph
"A force is applied to stretch a spring. The graph shows how the length of the spring varies with the applied force." The stiffness is: A the area between the graph and the x-axis; B the area between the graph and the y-axis; C the gradient of the graph; D 1/gradient of the graph.

### Recurring mark-scheme phrasing requirements

- Definitions must be exact. Centre of gravity: "Point through which weight may be taken to act" (Oct23) / "The point at which the weight of an object is considered to act" (Jan24) — "weight acts at that point" is rejected in both ERs. Upthrust: "An upwards force caused by the displacement of fluid (by an object) Or (a force equal and opposite to) the weight of fluid displaced (by an object)." Conservation of linear momentum needs BOTH halves: "total momentum before = total momentum after" AND "when no external force acts / closed or isolated system". Equilibrium: "No resultant force" + "No resultant moment (about any point)" — the word "resultant" is required (Jan24 ER: "clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment" alone did not score).
- Stokes' law conditions (asked in both sessions, 2 marks each): "small spherical object / spherical object moving at low speed" + "laminar flow [allow non-turbulent flow]" (Oct23); "moving at low speed" + "laminar flow / flow not turbulent" (Jan24, where "small sphere" was given in the stem so earned nothing). "The object must be laminar" was explicitly insufficient — laminar must describe the flow.
- Method marks are written as "Use of <equation>" — the correct equation selection earns the mark even if arithmetic slips. Dependent marks are flagged "(dependent on MP1)" (Oct23 Q12(b)(i)); error-carried-forward is flagged "[ecf from (a)(i)]" (Oct23 Q13(a)(ii), Q15(b)(ii); Jan24 Q12(b), Q18(c)(ii)).
- "Allow" examples: graph-read tolerances — "T = 70 N (allow range of 65 to 75 N)"; "η = 0.046 Pa s [allow 0.044–0.048]"; "Density at 25 km = 0.05 kg m⁻³ (range 0.040–0.050)"; "Energy = 0.048 J (allow 0.0425–0.053 J)". Alternative routes are credited: "Allow use of any valid suvat method"; "Allow approaches that work backwards from 35 W…"; the EITHER/OR routes in Jan24 Q17(c) and Q18(c)(i).
- "Reject"-style notes: "Do not accept use of equations of motion" (Jan24 Q14(a)(ii), where energy conservation was mandatory); consolation marks "If no other mark scored, allow 1 mark for…" (Oct23 Q18(b)(i); Jan24 Q13 density-of-diver route; Oct23 Q15(b)(ii)).
- "Show that" values are quoted with the value obtained from the show-that number, e.g. "[Show that value gives 25.6 m s⁻¹]", "(show that answer gives 30°)", "(show that answer gives 81 N)" — candidates may use the given approximate value in later parts and still score via ecf.
- "Deduce whether…" questions demand a numerical comparison PLUS a conclusion: "P = 28 (W), which is not equal to 35 (W), so is not consistent" (Oct23 Q14(b)); "26.9 m³ < 50 m³ so yes" (Oct23 Q17(b)(ii)); "η is 0.94 (Pa s) ≈ 0.93 (Pa s) valid conclusion based on comparison of 0.94 with value supplied in question" (Jan24 Q15(b)(ii)); "E = 107 (GPa) ≈ 106 (GPa) so material is brass" (Jan24 Q17(c)).
- The two 6-mark extended-response questions (both marked with an asterisk) use the identical IC + linkage table: 6 IC points → max 4 IC marks + 2 linkage marks; 5 IC → 3+2; 4 IC → 3+1, etc. Sustained, linked reasoning is rewarded separately from the physics points.
- Re-stating information given in the stem earns nothing (Oct23 ER Q12(b)(i), Q16(a); Jan24 ER Q15(a)) — e.g. "the mass of each glider is the same, so the velocity after the collision is half the velocity before…" was credited with no marks because both facts were given.

2. ER Oct23 Q12(b)(ii): "some answers referred to the forces cancelling out, rather than stating that the forces were equal in size and opposite in direction."
3. ER Oct23 Q13(b): "Many candidates described the angle of the displacement of the ball when it hit the ground, instead of the angle of the velocity of the ball when it hit the ground."
4. ER Oct23 Q14(a): "It was reasonably common for candidates to draw a free-body force diagram rather than a vector diagram with vector arrows drawn 'tip to tail'."
5. ER Oct23 Q15(a): "Many candidates instead stated that the centre of gravity is the point where weight acts, which was not sufficient to score the mark."
6. ER Oct23 Q17(c): "A lot of responses described what happened until the forces became balanced and the balloon reached terminal velocity."
7. ER Oct23 Q18(b)(i): "Most candidates scored no marks on this question. The most common reason for this was that the answers did not often refer to the graph, and therefore could not explain the shape of the graph."
8. ER Oct23 Q18(b)(ii): "A surprisingly high proportion of candidates interpreted v in the equation F = 6πηρv [sic] as a volume rather than a velocity."
9. ER Jan24 Q11(b): "A lot of students did not convert the unit of extension from centimetres to metres."
10. ER Jan24 Q12(a): "it was reasonably common for students to incorrectly round their calculated answer to 0.138 s instead of 0.139 s."
11. ER Jan24 Q14(a)(i): "Many students referred to constant velocity rather than constant acceleration, and few students realised that the relevant point about the shape of the slide was that the gradient varies."
12. ER Jan24 Q17(b)(i): "Common incorrect answers included wearing a helmet, gloves or shoes."
13. ER Jan24 Q17(b)(ii): "Answers to this question often demonstrated a lack of knowledge about the meaning of the terms resolution, precision, accuracy and uncertainty."
14. ER Jan24 Q18(c)(ii): "Many students calculated the complimentary angle, giving 62° instead of 28°."
15. ER Jan24 Q18(c)(iii): "This was the most challenging question on the paper, with many students struggling to express their answers, and many vague statements which were insufficient to score marks."

Cross-session error themes: (i) incomplete definitions (CoG, upthrust, momentum conservation, equilibrium, Stokes conditions) in both reports; (ii) graph questions answered without referring to the graph (Oct23 Q18(b)(i)) or without noticing graph detail (Jan24 Q17(c) non-origin line; Q16(a) ms vs s axis; Oct23 Q18(b)(ii) single point instead of gradient); (iii) missing comparisons in "deduce" questions (Jan24 report introduction: "it was common for students who calculated an appropriate value to just state their answer rather than comparing their calculated value with a value given in the question"); (iv) unit/power-of-ten and rounding slips (Oct23 Q13(a) 0.71 vs 0.72 s; Jan24 Q12(a) 0.138 vs 0.139 s; cm→m); (v) confusing decreasing acceleration with decreasing velocity (Oct23 Q17(c)); (vi) using the wrong given value (Oct23 Q14(b): 121 N instead of 150 N) or the wrong physics tool (SUVAT where energy conservation is required, Jan24 Q14(a)(ii)).

### Question formats that repeat across the two sessions, and session comparison

Formats appearing in both sessions:
- Projectile "show that the time ≈ …" then horizontal range with a = 0: Oct23 Q13 (horizontal launch, height 2.54 m, range 17.89 m) vs Jan24 Q12 (5.0° launch at 15.6 m s⁻¹, vertical velocity zero at impact). Both mark schemes credit "any valid suvat method" and apply ecf from the time to the distance.
- Stokes' law two-part structured question: conditions (2 marks, near-identical MS) + a terminal-velocity viscosity calculation (4–6 marks). Oct23 gives the drag directly as "difference between upthrust and weight" (1.45 × 10⁻⁴ N) and requires the gradient of a displacement–time graph for v; Jan24 gives terminal velocity (0.0824 m s⁻¹) but requires the candidate to compute upthrust from ρVg and subtract it from weight before using F = 6πηrv — Jan24 is the harder variant.
- Hinged-object moments question with "show that" force: Oct23 Q15 (board + ladder, hinge + block, 8 marks) vs Jan24 Q18 (door on two hinges, 16 marks). Both need the CG distance (0.50 m ladder; w/2 = 0.425 m door), clockwise = anticlockwise moments about the hinge, and a follow-up force/angle calculation; Jan24 adds a component/trigonometry part and a qualitative "what if L decreases" part.
- Centre of gravity 1-mark definition in both papers (Oct23 Q15(a), Jan24 Q18(b)) — both ERs report the same failure ("weight acts" vs "appears/considered to act").
- Newton's 3rd law pairs: Oct23 Q12(b)(ii) (magnets on gliders) and Q16(a) (electromagnet vs weight); Jan24 *Q16(b) (ball vs skittle, combined with N2 as a 6-mark IC question).
- Conservation of momentum with a collision: Oct23 Q12 (equal masses stick, deduce law obeyed, 4 marks, conceptual) vs Jan24 Q16(c) (numeric 1D collision, find mass of ball, 3 marks).
- "Deduce whether" comparison questions: Oct23 Q14(b) (28 W vs 35 W), Oct23 Q17(b)(ii) (26.9 m³ vs 50 m³ burst limit, including reading a density–height graph); Jan24 Q15(b)(ii) (0.94 vs 0.934 Pa s), Q17(c) (107 vs 106 GPa, identify brass from a table).
- Force–extension graph area / elastic strain energy: Oct23 Q16(b)(ii) (annotate graph, compare ΔE(el) with ΔGPE) vs Jan24 Q11(b) (hysteresis loop area between loading and unloading lines = energy causing heating). Both ERs report candidates failing to annotate graphs and treating curves as single straight lines for E = ½FΔx.
- MCQ pairs: scalar/vector table (Q1 Oct23 vs Q2 Jan24); SI unit of a derived quantity (momentum Oct23 Q2, stress Jan24 Q1); a motion-graph interpretation item (s–t of falling ball Oct23 Q4; P–t of cyclist Jan24 Q4; a–t area Jan24 Q10).
- Both papers carry exactly one asterisked 6-mark extended answer (Oct23 *17(c) balloon velocity; Jan24 *16(b) Newton's laws), both marked with the same IC/linkage table.

Session comparison: Oct 2023 leans towards scientific/industrial contexts (rocket launch, air-track gliders, tennis-ball machine, gym cable machine, loft ladder, electromagnet and steel cable, weather balloon, falling ball-bearing); Jan 2024 leans towards everyday/toy contexts (rubber band, darts, cartesian diver, child's slide, ball-bearing in glycerol, skittles, door hinges). Oct 2023 contains more definition recalls (CoG, upthrust, momentum principle, Stokes conditions, N3-pair reasons) and the unusual scaled-vector-diagram demand (Q14(a)); Jan 2024 pushes practical skills harder (micrometer checks, safety goggles, vernier resolution, non-origin force–extension line in the CP3 question) and has the more demanding moments question (16 marks with components and a qualitative extrapolation). Both sessions finish with materials (Oct23) or moments (Jan24) as the longest question, and both devote 10–13 marks to viscosity of a falling ball. Neither session examined Core Practical 1 (acceleration of free fall), spec 6 appeared only in Oct23, and spec 22 (efficiency) and spec 30 (stress–strain graphs) appeared only as single MCQs (Oct23 Q7, Jan24 Q9 respectively).
