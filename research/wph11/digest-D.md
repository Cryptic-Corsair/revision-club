### Notable questions

**Q11 (4) — recoil explanation.** "Max 4": "Initial momentum (of the child, ball and skateboard/total) is zero"; "Due to conservation of momentum, the total momentum before the ball is thrown = total momentum after the ball is thrown (so final total momentum is zero)"; "The momentum of the child/skateboard is equal to the momentum of the ball"; "The momentum of the child/skateboard is opposite in direction to the momentum of the ball"; "As the mass of the child/skateboard greater (than the mass of ball), the velocity (of the child/skateboard) will be lower". Notes: "(all symbols to be defined, 'mv' to be defined if used for momentum)".

**Q12 (4) — ramp efficiency.** MPs: "Use of sin θ = 2.0/15 Or use of θ = 7.7°"; "Use of Work done = FΔs Or use of ΔEgrav = mgΔh"; "Use of efficiency = useful energy output / total energy input (× 100 %)"; "Efficiency = 83 or 84 % so less than 90 % (MP4 dependent on scoring all points MP1 & 2 & 3)". Example: W₅₀ = 50 kg × 9.81 × 2.0 m × (2.0/15) = 130.8 J; W₈ = 8.0 kg × 9.81 × 2.0 m = 157.0 J; efficiency = 130.8/157.0 × 100 % = 83 %.

**Q15(a) (5) — forearm moments (estimation).** "Estimate of length of forearm 30 – 50 (cm)"; "Use of trig to determine the perpendicular component of the tension Or see Tsin70 Or see Tcos20"; "Use of moment = Fx with a corresponding force and distance"; "Use of the principle of moments"; "Value for T in range 85 N to 150 N (l = 30 cm, T = 85 N and l = 50 cm, T = 150 N)". Example (l = 0.40 m): (0.04 m × T × sin70°) = (0.31 m × 4.5 N) + (0.20 m × 15 N); T = 117 N. (b) (2): "The forearm is not uniform/symmetrical" / "The centre of gravity is not in the middle".

**Q16(b) (4) — CP3 Young modulus from gradient.** "Use of A = π(d/2)²"; "Calculate gradient of linear section (up to 3 × 10⁻³ m, 6.8 N) of graph Or use of a corresponding pair of points for F and Δx from the linear region"; "Use of σ = F/A and ε = Δx/l Or use of E = Fl/(AΔx) Or use of E = gradient × l/A"; "E = (1.2 – 1.3) × 10¹¹ Pa (MP4 conditional on scoring MP1 & MP2 & MP3)". Example: A = π(2.3 × 10⁻⁴ m/2)² = 4.15 × 10⁻⁸ m²; gradient = 6.5 N / 2.9 × 10⁻³ m = 2.2 × 10³ N m⁻¹; E = 2.2 × 10³ × 2.4 m ÷ 4.15 × 10⁻⁸ m² = 1.27 × 10¹¹ Pa. Part (a) (3): micrometer or "(vernier)digital calipers", "at different orientations and/or positions along the wire", take a mean. Part (c) linkage ICs include: "For long(er) wire, the extension will be large(r)"; "extension is proportional to the original length"; "For a thin(ner) wire, the extension will be large(r)"; "extension is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area"; "The percentage uncertainty in the extension/length will be lower"; "A small(er) load can be used with a long/thin wire".

**Q17(a)(i) (5) — climber fall factor.** "Use of fall factor = height fallen before the rope begins to stretch / total unstretched length of rope"; "Use of ε = Δx/x with x = 15.0 m"; "Use of ΔEgrav = mgΔh"; "Use of ΔEgrav = Eel with their Δx"; "Fmax = 14 000 (N)". Example: height fallen = 15.0 m × 0.8 = 12 m; Δx = 0.09 × 15.0 m = 1.35 m; ΔEgrav = 71 kg × 9.81 × 12 m = 8358 J (from fall) + 71 kg × 9.81 × 1.35 m = 940.3 J (from extension); 8358 + 940.3 = ½ × Fmax × 1.35 m; Fmax = 13 775 N. (a)(ii) (2): longer rope → "the climber would reach a higher velocity (just before the rope stretches)" hence "the climber's deceleration/force (as the rope stretches) would be greater". (b) (max 6): "Use of area under the graph to determine the stored energy"; "Energy = 800 J (new)"; "Energy = 700 J (old)"; "The old rope would absorb/store less energy"; "Use of F = kΔx to determine k (accept gradient of a tangent)"; "The old rope is not as stiff as the new rope Or The old rope extends more"; "The old rope would break at a smaller applied force/stress".

**Q18(a)(ii) (4) — raindrop terminal velocity (quadratic drag, not Stokes).** "Use of A = πr² and V = (4/3)πr³"; "Use of ρ = m/V and W = mg"; "Use of W = F"; "v = 6.5 – 7.0 m s⁻¹". Example: A = π × (0.002 m)² = 1.26 × 10⁻⁵ m²; V = 3.35 × 10⁻⁸ m³; m = 3.35 × 10⁻⁵ kg; W = 3.29 × 10⁻⁴ N; 3.29 × 10⁻⁴ N = 0.45 × 1.2 kg m⁻³ × 1.26 × 10⁻⁵ m² × v²; v = 6.96 m s⁻¹. Part (a)(i) definition: see §7.3.

### Marking-guidance stand-ins (verbatim from the Jun19 MS; identical boilerplate in Jun22–Jun25 MSs)

- "Bald (i.e. no working shown) correct answers score full marks unless in a 'show that' question."
- "If a 'show that' question is worth 2 marks then both marks will be available for a reverse working; if it is worth 3 marks then only 2 will be available."
- "A separate mark is not usually given for a unit but a missing or incorrect unit will normally mean that the final calculation mark will not be awarded." "There will be no unit penalty applied in 'show that' questions or in any other question where the units to be used have been given." Same unit error penalised "only once within one clip".
- Sig figs: too few penalised "in 'show that' questions where use of too few significant figures has resulted in the candidate not demonstrating the validity of the given answer".
- "The use of g = 10 m s⁻² or 10 N kg⁻¹ instead of 9.81 m s⁻² or 9.81 N kg⁻¹ will be penalised by one mark (but not more than once per clip). Accept 9.8 m s⁻² or 9.8 N kg⁻¹."
- Graphs: scale must "spread plotted points over more than half of each axis and is not an awkward scale e.g. multiples of 3, 7 etc."; "Points should be plotted to within 1 mm"; best-fit line must be "a thin continuous line".

### Notable questions (from ER commentary)

**Q13(b) — "show that" a = g sin θ.** ER: "Many were unable to show why the acceleration was the parallel component of the acceleration due to gravity in the absence of any frictional force. However, most were then able to continue to score marks by using the 'show that' value in parts (ii) and (iii). Several students persisted in using a = 9.81 m s⁻² in subsequent parts despite having a 'show that' value that they could use, demonstrating a fundamental misunderstanding of the physical situation."

**Q14(b) — gradient and Young modulus.** ER: "the line is straight and passes through the origin, so the whole line may be used to obtain the gradient, gradient = 40 ÷ 2.15 = 18.6. Many students chose arbitrary points along the line and obtained correct answers, but if the points encompassed less than half the drawn line only one mark could be awarded." Also: "Answers where the gradient was expressed as the spring constant, which is the gradient × 10³ N m⁻¹ were also accepted." On (b)(ii): "some algebraic skill was required to obtain the correct equation. Common errors here included mistakes in calculating the cross-sectional area, power of ten errors and units."

**Q17 — Stokes' law oil drop.** ER: "The first two parts of this question were intended to give students the hint that both weight and upthrust were required for part (iii). … some stopped when they had calculated the mass rather than the weight, and the most common error in (ii) was to use the wrong density, making the weight equal to the upthrust. In part (iii) there were many marks not scored due to careless rounding or truncation of values from (i) and (ii) … Several students used a set piece formula for the velocity; this is acceptable if correctly done, but a possibly dangerous approach if incorrect quantities (particularly derived values for the drop radius) are substituted."

**Q18 — power on a slope.** ER: "The most common error in this item was attempting to find some component of the weight along the slope. Students who knew the composite formula for power (power = force × velocity) were able to answer this question easily, or else derive the formula from given equations." On (c)(d): "some students attempted to add the KE also, which is not required as the stone is already moving. The efficiency was nearly always correctly calculated, with the mark being also awarded for correctly substituted errors carried forward. Students are encouraged to be suspicious if they obtain an efficiency of more than 100 %."

**Q19(a) — parasol toppling.** ER: "Very many candidates were able to show that the anticlockwise moment due to the wind initially exceeds the clockwise moment due to the weight of the parasol, but it was extremely rare to see the additional point that the overall anticlockwise moment does not decrease with the angle of tilt, but in fact increases, so there is no possibility of the parasol ceasing to topple once it has started. Marks were also not scored here when students stated a conclusion without explicitly comparing relevant quantities."

### ER highlights (verbatim)

- "students were expected to apply abstract principles of mechanics to contexts they should have studied as well as new or more unfamiliar contexts."
- "A recurring theme in questions where a conclusion needed to be drawn or explained was not showing a comparison of a calculated result with the condition that it needed to satisfy. This applied to Q13(b) and Q16(c) and Q19(a)."
- "Final answers must be correctly rounded, not truncated, and truncated values in multi-stage calculations will not generally yield the required value for the final mark. It is advisable that students should use calculators to retain all significant figures for values carried forward and only round answers for the final line."
- Q11: "Most were able to calculate the increase in kinetic energy correctly … but majority did not realise that the total energy available was the sum of the kinetic energy and work done."
- Q12(a): "A majority forgot to include the condition, and a number did not make it clear enough that they were referring to a system rather than a single object. Some confused momentum with moment."
- Q12(b): "some gave no indication that the direction of motion was reversed. A minus sign is sufficient to show this."
- Q13(a): "the most common misconception being that there was a force acting parallel to the plane. Students should understand that if they are told they can ignore friction there should not be any arrow to indicate a frictional force."

- Q15: "most have attempted to answer this question about forces in terms of energy. There were no marks available for discussions of energy … To score marks in a linkage question it is imperative that students read the question very carefully, so that they answer the actual question, not simply recite a set narrative."
- Q17(a)(i): "Laminar flow has a precise definition which needs to be learned."
- Q17(a)(ii): "It is not necessary for Stokes' law to apply in order for the speed to be constant, so mentions of small spheres, laminar flow and temperature were all irrelevant."

## 7. Cross-session pattern analysis

### 7.1 June-paper style, and what the reports say

None of these six reports explicitly contrasts June with January series (the January papers are separate documents, outside this digest's sources). What the June/October reports do say, repeatedly, is what kind of paper these are:

- Oct21/Jun22/Jun23 (near-identical boilerplate): "This paper was concerned with the physics of forces, including gravitational forces, tension, reaction, and forces in fluids due to drag and upthrust as well as the effects of forces on the motion of objects in one and two dimensions. ### 7.4 Top candidate errors across the six examiner reports (verbatim)

1. No explicit comparison in "deduce" questions — Oct21: "A recurring theme in questions where a conclusion needed to be drawn or explained was not showing a comparison of a calculated result with the condition that it needed to satisfy." (Echoed by every later session; Jun25: "The most likely reason for a student only scoring two marks was because they did not compare their calculated value for d with the 3.0 m given in the question.")
2. Truncation/rounding — Oct21: "Final answers must be correctly rounded, not truncated, and truncated values in multi-stage calculations will not generally yield the required value for the final mark."
3. Forces question answered with energy — Oct21 Q15: "most have attempted to answer this question about forces in terms of energy. There were no marks available for discussions of energy".
4. Momentum conservation missing the condition and the "system" — Oct21 Q12(a): "A majority forgot to include the condition, and a number did not make it clear enough that they were referring to a system rather than a single object."
5. Not using the "show that" value later — Oct21 Q13(b): "Several students persisted in using a = 9.81 m s⁻² in subsequent parts despite having a 'show that' value that they could use".
6. Limit of proportionality vs elastic limit — Oct21 Q14(a): "Many have confused this limit with elastic limit, and a number included definitions of both thus demonstrating that they did not understand the distinction."
7. Elastic-limit definition incomplete — Jun22 Q12(a): "many candidates neglected to mention that for elastic behaviour, the return to original length occurs after the load is removed."
8. Reverse-working a "show that" — Jun22 Q17(a)(i): "a reverse working will not usually score full marks in a 'show that' question, and also that the result of a calculation must be shown to one more significant figure than the 'show that' value." (Jun24 Q13(a): "The reverse-engineering approach therefore limited candidates to a maximum of 2 marks.")
9. Projectile time-doubling — Jun22 Q18(b): "The most common incorrect approach was to find the time to maximum height and double it, giving a time that is too long." (Jun23 Q14: same error yielding "half the range".)
10. Energy question misread as ΣF = ma — Jun23 Q13(b): "Candidates who attempted a ΣF = ma approach scored no marks as, again, there is no indication that the forces were constant."
11. Yield point definition not learned — Jun23 Q20(a): "very few candidates had learned this definition which needed to include two elements to score the mark, the idea of a large increase in strain and that the strain must be plastic."
12. Ignoring given data (breaking stress unchanged) — Jun23 Q20(c): "Most candidates did not realise that breaking stress was unchanged, and often stated that greater stress was required to break the new cable despite this being clearly contradicted by the question."
13. "Floats when weight < upthrust" misconception — Jun24 Q12(b): "a clear misconception demonstrated by many candidates that the iceberg would float if weight is less than upthrust, rather than floating when weight is equal to upthrust."
14. Diameter used as radius — Jun25 Q16(b): "they have used the diameter of the ball instead of the radius, so have ended up with an answer double what it should be." (Jun24 Q16(b): "Some candidates treated the diameter as a radius when calculating the cross-sectional area.")
15. Wrong energy accounting — Jun25 Q14: "it was far less common for students to determine that the total energy input to the battery was the change in kinetic energy minus the change in gravitational potential energy"; Jun25 Q15(a): "relatively common for candidates to multiply the change in force by the change in compression, which was incorrect physics".

Runners-up worth teaching from: "at equilibrium the moment is zero" instead of the sum (Jun24 Q18(b)); momentum "does not change at all" in a collision (Jun25 Q18(c)); upthrust described where air resistance is meant (Jun25 Q20(d)); answering the pre-parachute part for a post-parachute question (Jun25 Q20(d)); bare "every action has an equal and opposite reaction" (Jun24 Q15(c)); vertical/horizontal components instead of parallel/perpendicular to the rail (Jun25 Q19(b)(ii)); two straight lines instead of a curve on the v–t sketch (Jun25 Q20(b)); sphere volume with r² or missing 4/3 (Jun25 Q18(a)); stopping at mass instead of weight (Jun25 Q11, Jun23 Q17(a)(ii), Oct21 Q17(b)); giving two Stokes' conditions when one was asked, one wrong (Jun22 Q19(b)(iii)).

### 7.5 Calculation marks: ECF, sig figs, units, "show that"

**ECF** is generous and explicitly labelled in the MS: Jun22 Q17(a)(iii) "ΣF = 1.76 × 10² N (ecf from (a)(i))"; Jun22 Q18(b)/(c) "[Allow ecf from (a)]", "(Allow ecf from (b) with correct conclusion based on student's value)"; Jun23 Q13(b) "D = 8.0 × 10⁸ J (ecf from (a))"; Jun23 Q17(a)(ii) "Weight of buoy = 2500 N (ecf from (a)(i))"; Jun24 Q11(b) "2.8 × 10⁸ J (ecf from (a))"; Jun24 Q17(b)/(c) "(allow ecf from 17(a) and/or 17(b))"; Jun23 Q16(b)(ii) "Valid conclusion by comparison of relevant student values (ecf from (b)(i))". Jun24 ER Q17(c): "This mark could be scored even if mistakes had been made in the previous calculations." Oct21 ER Q18(d): efficiency "also awarded for correctly substituted errors carried forward". Jun25 ER Q18(b): an incorrect mass from (a) still allowed full marks "because the mass cancels out of the equations".

**Sig figs.** Standing MS rule: too many sig figs not penalised if the answer rounds to the MS value; too few is fatal only in "show that" questions, "where one more significant figure than the value in the question is needed"; practical-skills questions may demand a specific number. ER enforcement: Jun24 Q11(a) "the answer needed to be given correctly to at least one more significant figure than the 'show that' value given in the question"; Jun24 Q15(a) and Q17(a) similar; Jun24 Q11(a) records candidates "truncated their calculated answer from 417 × 10⁶ to 410 × 10⁶ instead of correctly rounding it to 420 × 10⁶".

**Units.** Standing rule: "a missing or incorrect unit will normally mean that the final calculation mark will not be awarded"; no penalty in "show that" questions or where units are given; the same unit error penalised only once per question; complex gradient units may be excused ([no ue]). Hard instances: Jun24 Q18(a) "Maximum 4 marks if incorrect / no unit with answers"; Jun22 Q12(b)(i) "A ratio of two quantities with the same unit does not have a unit"; Jun25 Q11 unit "omitted, or a wrong unit of kg … instead of N"; Jun25 Q17(b) "an incorrect unit such as N m⁻¹"; Jun23 General "some were losing the marks by neglecting to state units where required. This applied to Q16(b)(ii), Q19(b)(ii) and Q20(b)(iii)". Strain has no unit and may be a percentage. Also: "The use of g = 10 m s⁻² or 10 N kg⁻¹ instead of 9.81 m s⁻² or 9.81 N kg⁻¹ will be penalised by one mark (but not more than once per clip). Accept 9.8."

**"Show that" strategy.** "Bald (i.e. no working shown) correct answers score full marks unless in a 'show that' question"; "If a 'show that' question is worth 2 marks then both marks will be available for a reverse working; if it is worth 3 marks then only 2 will be available" (Jun25 MS repeats this); quote the final value to at least one more sig fig than given (see above); then use the given rounded value in later parts (Oct21 Q13(b) penalty for reusing 9.81). Jun24 Q13(a) shows the reverse-working trap live: candidates who "took the time of 1.15 [s] and used it to show the distances" were capped at 2 of 3 marks.

**Other conventions.** Method marks survive conversion errors: "use of the formula means that the candidate demonstrates substitution of physically correct values, although there may be conversion errors e.g. power of 10 error"; "recall of the correct formula will be awarded when the formula is seen or implied by substitution". Alternative methods are routinely credited (Jun23 Q12(a)(ii) tangent OR suvat; Jun22 Q18(b) quadratic OR two-stage; Jun25 Q15(a) ½FΔx twice OR area under graph OR via k; Jun23 Q16(b)(ii) lists six acceptable target quantities). Conclusions must compare like with like — Jun25 ER: "a numerical comparison … can often be achieved using symbols such as <, >, =, or ≈, or by describing the comparison using words". Sanity-checking is recommended (Jun24 Q16(b): an extension "greater than the size of a typical laboratory" should trigger re-checking; Jun25 Q18(a): "a mass of 229 kg should send up a warning signal"), as is suspicion of efficiencies above 100 % (Oct21 Q18(d)). Graph marks: axes labelled with quantity and unit; scales spread "over more than half of each axis", no "awkward scale e.g. multiples of 3, 7"; points within 1 mm; "a thin continuous line" best fit. Drawn motion graphs earn shape marks (Jun19 Q13(a) "A positive constant velocity from 0 to 42 s and the same negative constant velocity from 48 s to 90 s"; Jun23 Q12(b) "Straight line from 0 to (1.5, −[magnitude from (a)(ii)])"; Jun25 Q20(b) "Curved line … with negative gradient decreasing in magnitude" + "Horizontal line after 12 seconds"). Scale vector diagrams need scaled, labelled, arrowed lines and a stated range (55 ± 2 N; 400 N, 390–410 N), with trigonometry-only answers capped (Jun22: "[Correct answer from trigonometry scores MP4 only]").
